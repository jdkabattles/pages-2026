# Marketing notes — options to decide, NOT live listings

All figures below are third-party estimates from web search results, NOT
Jonathan's results, sales, or earnings. Treat every figure as unverified
for this project until it is checked against the source at launch time.

## What people pay for

- Short guides and ebooks: earlier research put short guides at $7–$39,
  and one later source put PDFs/ebooks generally at $17–$97. The
  pronunciation guide in this set is proposed at $19–$29, to decide only.
- Templates: earlier research noted templates at $19–$79 in one source;
  later sources put templates and swipe files at $27–$197. The freelancer
  page in this set is a template *guide*, not a finished template file —
  its price is still to decide.
- Courses: later sources put mini-courses at $47–$197 and longer courses
  higher. No course is included in this set.
- Paid newsletters: recurring subscriptions, often cited at $5–$15/month
  in one source. One 2026 roundup cited 5 million+ paid subscriptions on
  Substack and $19 million in paid-subscription revenue on Beehiiv in
  2025 (up from $8 million in 2024) — again, third-party platform
  figures, not Jonathan's, and not a promise for a new newsletter.
- Categories that recur across sources: business/career, health,
  education and languages, personal development, test preparation,
  templates, guides, courses, and paid newsletters. This set leans on
  languages (pronunciation), business/career skills (blog audience,
  freelancer pricing), travel planning (Japan), and money terms
  (referral checklist) — all specific, single-question pages, not
  generic "make money" pages.

## Where to market (options to decide)

1. Search intent first. One page = one buyer query, answer first, honest
   title, fast mobile page. That is the main channel for evergreen
   static pages; no marketplace listing replaces it.
2. Email list, built from readers who found a page useful — not from a
   form on a draft page. These pages collect no addresses.
3. For newsletters specifically: Substack or Beehiiv, as options to
   decide. Check their current fees and terms at launch; do not copy
   fee figures from this note.
4. For templates and guides specifically: Gumroad, Payhip, or Etsy, as
   options to decide. Gumroad is described in sources as easy for simple
   downloads but taking a percentage per sale; Etsy suits templates and
   printables but adds listing and transaction fees. Check current fees
   before choosing — no listing exists on any of these platforms yet.

## How to market these 5 pages

- One query per page, answered in the first paragraph. Do not blur the
  pronunciation page into a general English course, or the checklist
  into a referral-code directory.
- Honest titles and promises: intelligibility, not native speech; a
  checklist, not a guaranteed bonus; a route and reading skills, not a
  priced itinerary; a template structure, not financial advice.
- Large, clear visuals and fast mobile loading. These drafts use simple
  inline SVG heroes; replace the placeholder hero image path with a real,
  properly sized image before launch, with alt text.
- One next step per page, no competing asks, no email form on a draft.
- Do not add testimonials, sales counts, rankings, or earnings until
  they are real, measured, and provable — and never for a product that
  has not launched.
